Wireframe · v0

Career Card & Free Agent page

A career page that borrows the grammar of a ballplayer's page: teams become companies, seasons become years, positions become job titles, and the gap after a layoff becomes free agency. Each stint is a playing card that flips to reveal the details. The job-hunt timeline stays as a second view of the same data.

Blue dashed boxes are wireframe placeholders. The card in board 2 is closer to real so the idea reads; everything about it is up for discussion. Names and figures are examples.

  1. Masthead reads like the top of a player page: name in condensed caps, then position · city · seasons. The status pill is the one loud element on the page.
  2. Cards / Timeline toggle switches the main area between the shelf and the timeline. Same data, two views. Status pill and stat line stay put.

Front is the poster: the company as a chunky wordmark (the "ORIOLES" treatment), a big art area, the person's name, and role + years in a one-line caption. The corner number is the stint's order in the career, the way a card number sits in a set.

A
Art area. No photo needed. Options: monogram, company logo if you have rights, or a flat illustration in the line-drawn style of the clubs cards. Colour per company (team colours).
B
Position badge. The baseball-on-the-card "CF" mark becomes a short role code: PDL, CAM, TL.
C
Back header repeats the team and the city, like the card back's team bar.
D
Seasons table. Every title held at that company, with years, in stat-table form. This is where promotions live.
E
Highlights. Three to five resume bullets. Truncate with "more" if long; the card must stay one screen.
F
Skills tiles in the letter-grid style. Same skill vocabulary across cards so the scouting report can roll them up.
G
Footer. Why the stint ended ("traded", "left for", "role eliminated") and the card number in the set.

Free Agent card uses the same shell, inverted: dark ground, red wordmark, "Open to" roles on the front; on the back the job-hunt figures, target roles, and a link into the timeline.

  1. Three visual directions are on the table, all from your references: the hand-drawn red line of the clubs cards, the chunky riso of the Grandsans card, and the 70s Topps of the Orioles card. The mock leans Topps for the front and riso for the back. Pick one family and I'll commit to it.
  2. Card ratio is the real one, 2.5 × 3.5. Shelf cards are ~130px wide; a flipped card enlarges to ~260px so the back is legible. On phones the flipped card takes the screen.
  3. Flip is a plain CSS 3D transform; works without a library. Keyboard: Enter flips, Esc closes.
Open questionPublic or private? A shareable career page wants a stable URL and no personal job-hunt details on it. A private one can show the whole free-agency log. Sketch assumes one page with the job-hunt detail behind the Timeline view, and a "share" mode that hides it.

Before building

Decisions that change the build

1 · Card unitOne card per stint (company + a run of related roles). Promotions on the back.Alternative: one card per role. More cards, thinner backs.
2 · Visual familyTopps-70s front + riso back, as mocked.Alternative: the all-red hand-drawn clubs look, which is quieter and prints well.
3 · AudiencePrivate by default, with a share mode that hides the free-agency log and shows the cards and scouting report only.Alternative: two separate pages.
4 · DataStints entered by hand in the drawer (there are only a few). Job-hunt events keep the tracker import. Resume bullets pasted per stint.Alternative: import a LinkedIn export to seed stints.
5 · ArtMonogram + team colours to start; illustration or logo later.Keeps the first build free of image hosting.
6 · NameWorking title only. "Career Card" and "Free Agent" are both placeholders.

Suggested build order once these are settled: stint data model and card shelf with flip → masthead and stat line → timeline spans → share mode → scouting report and the rest of the fold.
